# GOLD-16: DTI Severely Over Ceiling Triggers Decline

## Decision

**DECLINE** | Risk Grade: **D3** | Adverse Action Required: **Yes**

## Reasoning Chain

1. **Borrower eligibility**: Uma Erikson, 28-year-old US citizen, applying for REFI of $176,292.47. No cosigner.

2. **Credit evaluation**: Borrower FICO 731 with established file (190 months oldest tradeline). Clean delinquency history with zero 30/60/90-day marks. Low revolving utilization at 15%. On credit score alone, this borrower would qualify.

3. **Income and DTI -- HARD STOP**: Contract nurse earning $56,256.82 annually. Monthly debt service of $3,234 plus housing of $749 against monthly gross of $4,688 produces a DTI of 69.0%. This is dramatically above the maximum ceiling of 43% per POL-002 EDU-UW-001.

4. **RC-003 application**: DTI of 69.0% triggers RC-003 (excessive debt-to-income ratio) per POL-009. This is a hard decline trigger regardless of other credit factors.

5. **Residual income**: Only $1,453/month in residual income after all obligations, which is critically insufficient to absorb additional loan payments from the refinance.

6. **Existing loans**: Two existing loans (both current, both with payoff statements received). The loans themselves are performing, but the income cannot support the consolidated payment.

7. **Risk grade**: D3 reflects the severe DTI overage. Despite a good FICO score, DTI is the binding constraint.

## Policy Citations

- POL-011 EDU-REFI-001 (refinance underwriting)
- POL-009 RC-003 (excessive DTI)
- POL-002 EDU-UW-001 (underwriting standards -- DTI ceiling)

## Documents Requested

None. The issue is insufficient income relative to debt, not missing documentation.

## Conditions

None. Hard decline. Borrower should consider reducing the refinance amount, increasing income, or adding a cosigner to bring DTI within acceptable limits.
